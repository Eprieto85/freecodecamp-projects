Leap Year Calculator

The Gregorian calendar's leap year rule is a classic compound boolean problem. Used in every scheduling and date library ever written.

// isLeapYear(year)
🌙
2024 is a leap year.
divisible by 4 AND not by 100
// The three-rule algorithm
RULE 1
year % 4 === 0
Must be divisible by 4. Base condition for all leap years.
—
RULE 2
year % 100 !== 0
Century years (1800, 1900) are NOT leap years, unless…
—
RULE 3
year % 400 === 0
Exception: years divisible by 400 ARE leap years (2000, 2400).
—
// Notable years — click to check

💡 Why it matters — this exact compound boolean logic ((year % 4 === 0 && year % 100 !== 0) || year % 400 === 0) lives inside JavaScript's Date engine, Python's calendar module, Java's LocalDate, and every scheduling system in the world. Getting it wrong caused the Y2K leap-year bug in many enterprise systems.
